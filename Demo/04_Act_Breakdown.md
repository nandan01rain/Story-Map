# THE SOUTHERN WING — CHAPTER BREAKDOWN (DEMO)

*Per chapter: **Purpose** · **Events** · **Character & reactions** · **Setup/payoff** · **Ends on**. POV grammar: third-person limited throughout, past tense, rotating between Sunny, Nakulan, and Sridevi — except Ch 0 (Nagavalli, flashback) and Ch 16 (Ganga, the one time we enter her).*

---

# ACT ONE

## CH 0 — WHAT THE COURT REMEMBERS
**POV:** Nagavalli (flashback) · **Status:** [PLANNED]
**When:** 150 years before the story, Thanjavur and Madampalli.

**Purpose:** Plant the true history in the reader's hands before the family's sanitized legend is ever told, so every later retelling of the legend reads with dramatic irony.

**Events**
- Thanjavur court, Nagavalli performing; Ramanathan watching from the margins — established as already her partner, not a later "seduction."
- The Karanavar's visit; the transaction that removes her from the court, told plainly, without romance.
- Arrival at Madampalli; the Thekkini as a gift that is also a cage.
- Ramanathan's cottage, the secret visits.
- The night: the Karanavar discovers them, goes to the cottage first — controlled, methodical, not a crime of passion — then to the Thekkini.
- Chapter ends before her death is shown directly; cut on the anklets.

**Character & reactions:** Nagavalli's clear-eyed awareness of her own situation throughout — never a passive victim in tone, even though she has no power in the situation.
**Setup/payoff:** Sets up the true-history/legend gap (paid off Ch 9); the anklets (paid off Ch 3 and Ch 13); the damaged portrait (paid off Ch 4).
**Ends on:** The anklets, still.

## CH 1 — THE HOUSE THAT KEEPS ITS DOORS LOCKED
**POV:** Nakulan · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** Establish the present-day family, the legend as the family tells it, and Nakulan's reasons for insisting on the visit despite warnings.

**Events**
- Arrival at Madampalli; the extended family already assembling, unable to let the couple go alone.
- Valyammai's formal warning about the Thekkini, told in full — the family version of the legend (Story Bible §1), delivered as fact.
- Ganga's visible, performative skepticism in front of the family.
- A quiet aside: Nakulan's own reason for insisting on staying here — refusing to let the house's reputation run his life.
- First hint of Ganga's "far-away moods," read by Nakulan as ordinary tiredness from travel.

**Character & reactions:** The family's fear is played straight, not comic; Ganga's skepticism should read as genuine but slightly too emphatic.
**Setup/payoff:** The legend (payoff Ch 9); "far-away moods" (payoff Ch 10).
**Ends on:** The locked door of the Thekkini, seen from outside for the first time.

## CH 2 — FAR-AWAY MOODS
**POV:** Nakulan · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** Deepen Nakulan and Ganga's marriage; establish his friendship with Sunny early so his later summons doesn't feel mechanical; plant Ganga's undisclosed grief.

**Events**
- A quiet domestic scene; Nakulan notices Ganga somewhere else entirely for several seconds, snapping back as if nothing happened.
- A brief, deflected exchange about a recent loss — kept vague enough not to name it outright (see Patch 1).
- Nakulan mentions Sunny by name, establishing the friendship and the fact that Sunny is "the person you call when something's wrong that a doctor here won't understand."
- Family gossip overheard: comparisons of Ganga to a relative with a history of "melancholy" — the red herring the family will lean on later.

**Character & reactions:** Nakulan's blind spot crystallizes here — he registers the moment and immediately explains it away.
**Setup/payoff:** Sunny's eventual summons; the melancholy red herring (paid off/debunked Ch 10); the undisclosed grief.
**Ends on:** Ganga's hand, briefly, resting on her own stomach without seeming to notice she's done it.

## CH 3 — THE KEY
**POV:** Sridevi · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** Ganga's transgression; introduce Sridevi properly and establish her as a careful observer from her very first POV chapter.

**Events**
- Sridevi witnesses Ganga persuading Alli to help steal the keys — recounts it faithfully, without embellishment, already the book's most reliable narrator.
- The theft itself; the Thekkini's external door, used specifically so they needn't cross the main house.
- Inside: the anklets, the lamp, the cracked mirror — Ganga's reaction is curiosity, not fear, and specifically a *recognition* she can't explain to herself.
- They relock the room; nobody else knows yet.

**Character & reactions:** Alli's flattered nervousness; Sridevi's private disapproval, kept to herself rather than reported — the trait that will later make her look suspicious.
**Setup/payoff:** The anklets and mirror (payoff Ch 7, Ch 13); Sridevi's habit of quiet observation (payoff Ch 8, Ch 11).
**Ends on:** Ganga, alone a moment, whispering a phrase in Tamil she has no memory of having learned.

## CH 4 — THE EYES THAT FOLLOW
**POV:** Nakulan · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** First ambiguous incident; introduce the damaged portrait; escalate household unease without yet confirming anything supernatural.

**Events**
- The portrait gallery, connecting the main house to the Thekkini's outer door; a portrait of the historical Karanavar has a slashed or defaced section — nobody in the present generation remembers why or when.
- Household objects begin shattering inexplicably — staged so it's plausible as draft, structural settling, or (unconfirmed) something else.
- Valyammai's fear sharpens into open dread; she notices the Thekkini's lock has been disturbed.
- Ganga's mood shifts sharply and briefly at dinner — gone before anyone can name it.

**Character & reactions:** The household's unease becomes collective rather than individual — first "we" language about the haunting.
**Setup/payoff:** The damaged portrait (payoff Ch 9); shattering objects as an established "symptom" pattern (payoff Ch 10).
**Ends on:** Valyammai discovering the disturbed lock, saying nothing to anyone yet.

---

## CH 5 — WHAT THE PUJA COULDN'T FIX
**POV:** Nakulan · **Status:** [PLANNED] · *Act One climax*

**Purpose:** First real violence; crystallize the family's fear into open supernatural belief; end Act One on the decision to summon outside help.

**Events**
- The disturbed lock is discovered by the whole family; the theft is out.
- A traditional purification ritual/puja is performed to calm the house — fails visibly, worsening the mood rather than resolving it.
- That night: Ganga's saree catches fire near the household shrine's lamps — staged so it is plausible as an accident involving someone in a dissociative state near open flame, without confirming anything.
- Panic; the family's fear becomes explicit and collective — someone says the word "possessed" aloud for the first time, aimed uncertainly at Ganga, then redirected (wrongly) toward Sridevi because of her composure in the chaos, which reads to the frightened family as unnatural calm.
- Nakulan, shaken, decides to call Sunny.

**Character & reactions:** The pivot from private fear to public accusation; Sridevi's first taste of being looked at differently.
**Setup/payoff:** The saree fire as the pattern's most dangerous escalation so far; Sridevi's wrongful suspicion begins here, properly, for Act Two to develop.
**Ends on:** Nakulan on the phone, asking Sunny to come immediately. **End of Act One.**

# ACT TWO

## CH 6 — THE MAN FROM AMERICA
**POV:** Sunny · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** Introduce Sunny at full voice; establish his method; the Alli assault incident and Mahadevan's wrongful suspicion.

**Events**
- Sunny's arrival — wry, informal, immediately undercutting the household's dread with humor the family finds either refreshing or disrespectful depending on who you ask.
- His first read of the situation: treats it as a case, not a haunting, without dismissing the family's fear as stupid.
- That night: Alli is violently frightened/grabbed in a dim corridor; Mahadevan, found nearby, is accused — he protests his innocence loudly and is not believed.
- Sunny's private read of the scene doesn't match the accusation, though he says nothing yet.

**Character & reactions:** Mahadevan's genuine bewilderment plays as more convincing than the family credits it; Sunny quietly filing away a detail nobody else notices.
**Setup/payoff:** Mahadevan's wrongful suspicion (payoff Ch 10, when reframed as the "Ramanathan" projection); Sunny's noticed detail (payoff Ch 10).
**Ends on:** Sunny asking, mildly, whether anyone actually saw what happened, and getting no clear answer.

## CH 7 — MIDNIGHT IN THE THEKKINI
**POV:** Sunny · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** The book's most overtly "haunted" set piece, staged so it can be reread as clinical once the reader knows the truth; the Durgashtami threat is issued.

**Events**
- Midnight: singing and dancing in Tamil, coming from the re-locked Thekkini.
- Sunny goes to investigate rather than avoid it — adopts the vocal persona of the historical Karanavar (a calculated risk, explained in his own head as "give it something to talk to") and engages the voice in dialogue.
- The voice identifies itself as Nagavalli, names her grievance, and vows to decapitate the Karanavar on the coming night of Durgashtami.
- Sunny doesn't confirm or deny anything to the family afterward — buys himself time to investigate properly instead of reacting.

**Character & reactions:** Sunny's calm under a genuinely frightening scene, contrasted with his private uncertainty once alone.
**Setup/payoff:** The Durgashtami deadline (payoff Act Three); the vocal-persona trick (paid off again, deliberately, in Ch 12's ritual).
**Ends on:** Sunny alone in the corridor afterward, no longer treating this as a straightforward case.

## CH 8 — LOCKED IN HER OWN ROOM
**POV:** Sridevi · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** The poisoning subplot; Sridevi wrongly confined; establish this incident as mundane and separate from the dissociative pattern (per Story Bible §3).

**Events**
- An unknown party attempts to poison Nakulan's tea — caught before real harm is done.
- Sunny, still gathering information and buying time, publicly blames Sridevi and has her confined to her quarters — a calculated, uncomfortable choice on his part, not a genuine belief.
- Sridevi's private reaction: humiliation, then a decision to start keeping her own written account of events rather than simply enduring the suspicion.
- A brief, deliberately unresolved thread: the estate accountant (**[OPEN]** — needs a name before drafting) had a financial dispute with Nakulan's uncle days earlier, planted as a plausible mundane suspect for the poisoning without ever being confirmed on the page.

**Character & reactions:** Sridevi's composure under a second, worse accusation; the household fully convinced now that she is "the one."
**Setup/payoff:** Sridevi's written record (payoff Ch 10, when she hands it to Sunny); the accountant thread (deliberately left as texture, not resolved — [OPEN] whether to pay this off in a later patch).
**Ends on:** Sridevi, alone, starting her notes with the sentence: "I did not do this."

## CH 9 — WHAT EVOOR REMEMBERS
**POV:** Sunny · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** Fracture the legend; deliver the true 150-year-old history to both Sunny and the reader; deepen the thematic spine.

**Events**
- Sunny travels to Evoor, following a hunch from the vocal cadence and specific Tamil phrasing used by the "voice" — a detail nobody else thought to chase.
- Interviews a descendant of the household's old retainer family and consults a temple record.
- Receives the true history (Story Bible §1): the transaction, not a romance; Ramanathan not a seducer but her already-partner; the murders as methodical rather than a single crime of passion; the mysterious forty-years-later gap before the haunting reports began.
- Sunny connects the true history's details to specific phrases the "voice" used in Ch 7 — phrases that match the accurate record, not the sanitized family legend, which is the first hard clinical clue that whoever is speaking learned the story from somewhere more specific and detailed than the family's telling.

**Character & reactions:** Sunny's investigative satisfaction curdling into unease as the pieces start pointing somewhere he doesn't want them to point.
**Setup/payoff:** The true-history/legend gap (paid off from Ch 0); the phrase-matching clue (payoff Ch 10's diagnosis scene).
**Ends on:** Sunny, on the road back, asking himself who in the household could possibly know these specific, unrecorded details.

---

## CH 10 — THE DIAGNOSIS
**POV:** Sunny · **Status:** [PLANNED] · *Act Two climax*

**Purpose:** Sunny reaches the correct diagnosis privately; confides in Sridevi; the melancholy red herring is debunked; the true nature of Ganga's condition is laid out fully for the reader.

**Events**
- Sunny reviews everything: Nakulan's "far-away moods," the grand-aunt's childhood performances of the legend, the phrase-matching from Evoor, the pattern of incidents that are each independently explicable, Ganga's own hand-on-stomach tell from Ch 2.
- Diagnosis, laid out in full clinical honesty: Dissociative Identity Disorder, with a clear etiology — the childhood exposure, the accumulated present-day stress, an alternate identity that has adopted the Nagavalli persona wholesale, including details only explicable if Ganga absorbed the deep, accurate version of the story as a child (implicating the grand-aunt's performances, not supernatural knowledge).
- The melancholy-relative red herring is explicitly debunked — a different, unrelated family history that everyone had been pattern-matching onto Ganga out of fear, not evidence.
- Sunny confides fully in Sridevi, the only person he trusts with the truth; she shares her own written record in return, and it corroborates his read precisely.
- Sridevi agrees to help.

**Character & reactions:** Sunny's relief at being right sitting uneasily against the weight of what "right" now requires of him; Sridevi's vindication arriving privately, before it arrives publicly.
**Setup/payoff:** Every Act One/Two "clue" pays off here at once; sets up the need for a socially acceptable delivery mechanism (Ch 12).
**Ends on:** Sunny's realization that a clinical diagnosis alone will save no one — the family will never accept it on its own terms.

## CH 11 — WHAT SRIDEVI KNEW
**POV:** Sridevi · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** Public vindication; the elders summon the Namboodiri, setting up Act Three.

**Events**
- With Sunny's backing, Sridevi's written record and the true circumstances of the tea incident come out — she is publicly cleared.
- The family's collective relief and shame, handled without anyone grovelling extensively — a brisk, dignified correction rather than a big emotional scene, in keeping with the household's reserved register.
- Meanwhile, and separately, the elders — frightened and now doubly so with Durgashtami approaching — summon the renowned tantric expert, **Pullattuparam Brahmadathan Namboodiri**, over Sunny's mild private objection.
- Sunny recognizes the name; the two are old associates.

**Character & reactions:** Sridevi's relief is real but guarded — she does not fully trust the household's fear not to land on someone else next.
**Setup/payoff:** The Namboodiri's arrival (Act Three); Sunny and the Namboodiri's prior history (**[OPEN]** — needs fixing before drafting).
**Ends on:** The Namboodiri's palanquin/car arriving at the gate. **End of Act Two.**

# ACT THREE

## CH 12 — TWO TRADITIONS, ONE PLAN
**POV:** Sunny · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** The book's thematic centerpiece — Sunny and the Namboodiri's collaboration, worked out in full.

**Events**
- Sunny and the Namboodiri meet privately; Sunny lays out the clinical truth in full.
- The Namboodiri does not concede that Sunny's framework is "more true" than his own — he simply recognizes that his own tradition's technique for a spirit's "release" and Sunny's clinical plan for a controlled confrontation and exit event are, mechanically, close enough to run as one ritual.
- Together they design the Durgashtami ritual: a public, ceremonially correct exorcism that is secretly staged to give Ganga's alter the confrontation and "victory" it needs (a symbolic execution of the Karanavar) without anyone actually getting hurt.
- The mechanism is set: a lifelike straw dummy, a lever/swap mechanism, sacred ash and smoke as both ritual element and practical blind for the swap.

**Character & reactions:** Two men from opposed disciplines speaking past each other's frameworks while agreeing completely on the plan — the chapter should never resolve which of them is "right," by design.
**Setup/payoff:** Every physical element of the Ch 15 climax is planted here (the dummy, the lever, the ash/smoke).
**Ends on:** The Namboodiri's blessing of the plan, in ritual language that means something different to him than it does to Sunny — and both of them fine with that.

## CH 13 — THE NIGHT BEFORE
**POV:** Nakulan · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** Rising dread; Nakulan learns he is to be the "target"; a last quiet scene between Nakulan and Ganga before the crisis.

**Events**
- Preparations for the Durgashtami ritual across the household — the ceremonial dressing of the courtyard.
- Sunny and Sridevi brief Nakulan, gently, on what's actually going to happen and what his role requires of him — steady nerve, trust, and silence.
- A quiet scene between Nakulan and Ganga (her lucid self) — neither of them naming what's coming, both aware something is.
- Valyammai's private fear, handled with dignity — she has already lost one relative to this story, generations back (**[OPEN]** — ties to the Ch 9 forty-years-later gap; needs fixing whether this is literal family history or left ambiguous).

**Character & reactions:** Nakulan's fear reframed as active courage rather than passive helplessness — this is his one moment of agency in the climax.
**Setup/payoff:** The emotional stakes of Ch 15's swap; Valyammai's history (ties back to Story Bible §1's [OPEN] item).
**Ends on:** Nakulan and Ganga's hands, briefly touching, neither of them saying what they mean.

## CH 14 — DURGASHTAMI
**POV:** Sunny · **Status:** [PLANNED]

**Purpose:** The alter fully takes over; the family witnesses the ritual begin; the last beat before the swap.

**Events**
- Ganga fully submerges into the Nagavalli persona — staged so the transition itself is the book's most overt "possession" beat, deliberately, right before the reveal strips it of supernatural explanation for good.
- She follows Mahadevan into the ritual courtyard, perceiving him as Ramanathan.
- The Namboodiri, in full ritual voice, converses with the persona — promises her the Karanavar's death in exchange for her departure from Ganga's body, exactly as designed with Sunny.
- Nakulan is brought forward.

**Character & reactions:** The household's genuine, frightened belief that they are watching a real exorcism — nobody but Sunny, the Namboodiri, and Sridevi know otherwise; Mahadevan's discomfort at being cast, again, as someone else's ghost.
**Setup/payoff:** Everything from Ch 12 converges.
**Ends on:** The sword raised.

---

## CH 15 — THE SWAP
**POV:** Sunny · **Status:** [PLANNED] · *Climax*

**Purpose:** The physical climax; the alter's exit; the immediate aftermath.

**Events**
- The Namboodiri releases smoke and sacred ash into Ganga's face, momentarily blinding her — ritually justified, practically necessary.
- Sunny, in the same instant, works the lever, swapping Nakulan for a lifelike straw dummy dressed identically.
- The persona, still blind with fury, hacks the dummy to pieces, fully convinced the Karanavar is dead.
- Believing her revenge complete, the alter withdraws — Ganga collapses, and for the first time in the book, is simply herself, disoriented, exhausted, without memory of what just happened.
- The household witnesses what looks, to them, like a successful exorcism.

**Character & reactions:** The precision required of Sunny and the Namboodiri under real time pressure; Nakulan's relief once he understands he was never in physical danger from the ritual itself, only from what it required of everyone around him.
**Setup/payoff:** Every planted mechanism from Ch 12 pays off in sequence.
**Ends on:** Ganga, on the ground, breathing, herself.

---

## CH 16 — WHAT GANGA REMEMBERS
**POV:** Ganga (the only time) · **Status:** [PLANNED] · *Resolution*

**Purpose:** The one chapter inside Ganga's head; her recovery under Sunny's ongoing clinical care; the family's reconciliation; the ending beats (Sridevi/Sunny).

**Events**
- Ganga wakes with only fragments — not the alter's memories, but the accumulated exhaustion and the recent grief she'd been carrying underneath everything (Patch 1 material, if accepted).
- Sunny explains the clinical truth to her directly and gently — the first time the diagnosis is spoken plainly to the person it's actually about, rather than around her.
- A period of targeted therapy, compressed but not skipped — the book should resist a magic-instant-cure ending.
- The family's farewell to the couple — warm, a little sheepish about the fear they'd aimed at Sridevi and, at points, at Ganga herself.
- Before Sunny and Sridevi return their separate ways (his to the US, hers staying), he proposes — a scene the text should build as earned across their small collaborative moments rather than sudden.

**Character & reactions:** Ganga's ending register is deliberately not triumphant — tired, a little raw, beginning rather than finished; contrasts with the neat closure the household wants to feel.
**Setup/payoff:** Every earlier "far-away mood," hand-on-stomach, and performative-wit beat resolves here.
**Ends on:** Ganga and Nakulan leaving the estate; the Thekkini's door, standing open now, empty, ordinary. **End of Act Three. End of book.**
